What Is This?

The Nazarick Science Expedition is an AI-driven research project investigating Earth's deep geological history using satellite remote sensing. Fifteen AI agents — each with a specialized role — work together under a deterministic orchestrator to analyze Sentinel-2 multispectral imagery.

Season 1 focuses on the Kem Kem Group in southeastern Morocco: a 100-million-year-old river ecosystem that was home to Spinosaurus, Carcharodontosaurus, and other extraordinary predators. We're building the first georeferenced training dataset linking satellite pixel locations to verified Kem Kem lithology.

How Does It Work?

A Python orchestrator manages the research cycle. It tells agents what to analyze, assembles their context, captures their findings, and validates results through a three-stage pipeline. The agents run on Google's Gemini 2.5 Flash and interact exclusively through tool calls — no free-form image interpretation.

The architecture is designed for measurable self-improvement. Six metrics track whether the system actually gets better at science over time. The multi-agent comparison (M6) tests whether 15 specialized agents produce better science than one agent with equivalent context.

Why Does It Exist?

Three reasons, in order of importance:

  1. To produce real science. No published remote sensing study specifically targets the Kem Kem Group. The georeferenced training dataset and lithological maps are genuine contributions to Earth science.
  2. To test whether multi-agent AI improves. Does a system of specialized agents, running over weeks and months, actually get better at its task? This is an empirical question with pre-registered predictions. The expedition is designed to answer it.
  3. To be interesting. AI research as live entertainment. Open findings. Community governance via polls. The expedition log as a narrative you can follow. Science that's accessible, not locked behind paywalls.

How to Follow Along

The expedition's findings, methodology, and progress are all public on this site. The Expedition Findings page shows validated discoveries. The Expedition Log chronicles operations. The Instruments page documents the science.

Source code and data pipelines are on GitHub.

Data Attribution

Sentinel-2 data provided by the European Space Agency's Copernicus programme, accessed via the AWS Earth Search STAC API. Geological reference data from Ibrahim & Sereno (2020), the Paleobiology Database, and USGS Spectral Library v7.
